# Landmark Precedents in Indian Family & Matrimonial Jurisprudence

### Rajnesh v. Neha, (2021) 2 SCC 324
**Bench & Court:** Supreme Court of India (Indu Malhotra, J.)
**Core Holding:** Established nationwide uniform guidelines for determining maintenance in matrimonial proceedings. Mandated that both parties must file comprehensive Affidavits of Assets and Liabilities covering income, properties, debts, standard of living, and expenses. Overlapping maintenance claims across HMA, DV Act, and CrPC §125 must be adjusted and set off.

### Naveen Kohli v. Neelu Kohli, (2006) 4 SCC 558
**Bench & Court:** Supreme Court of India
**Core Holding:** Held that sustained course of abusive conduct, false criminal complaints, and public humiliation constitute mental cruelty of such gravity that human endurance cannot reasonably be expected to tolerate it. While irretrievable breakdown is not an express statutory ground under HMA §13, prolonged severe acrimony amounts to mental cruelty.

### Samar Ghosh v. Jaya Ghosh, (2007) 4 SCC 511
**Bench & Court:** Supreme Court of India
**Core Holding:** Formulated illustrative principles on mental cruelty: mere trivial irritations and normal wear and tear of marriage are not cruelty, but sustained coldness, willful denial of marital relations without valid medical justification, and prolonged separation coupled with hostility establish legal cruelty.

### Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja, (2020) 10 SCC 788
**Bench & Court:** Supreme Court of India (Ashok Bhushan, J.)
**Core Holding:** Overruled earlier narrow interpretations of "shared household" under the DV Act. Held that an aggrieved daughter-in-law has a statutory right of residence in a household where she lived in a domestic relationship, even if the property belongs exclusively to the father-in-law or mother-in-law.

### Gaurav Nagpal v. Sumedha Nagpal, (2009) 1 SCC 42
**Bench & Court:** Supreme Court of India
**Core Holding:** Reaffirmed that the paramount consideration in child custody disputes is the "welfare of the child" — encompassing moral, physical, emotional, and educational well-being — which supersedes pure proprietary claims or financial superiority of either parent.
