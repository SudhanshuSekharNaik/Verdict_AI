# Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) & Criminal Statutes

### BNS Section 103 — Murder
(1) Whoever commits murder shall be punished with death or imprisonment for life, and shall also be liable to fine.
(2) Murder requires the causing of death with the intention of causing death, or with the intention of causing such bodily injury as the offender knows to be likely to cause death, or if the act is done with the knowledge that it is so imminently dangerous that it must in all probability cause death.
(3) Statutory Elements: Actus Reus (causing death of human being), Mens Rea (intention to cause death or knowledge of imminent fatality), Absence of General or Special Exceptions.

### BNS Section 103 Exception 1 — Grave and Sudden Provocation
(1) Culpable homicide is not murder if the offender, whilst deprived of the power of self-control by grave and sudden provocation, causes the death of the person who gave the provocation or causes the death of any other person by mistake or accident.
(2) Essential Provocation Rubric:
- Prong 1 (Gravity): The provocation must be grave enough to cause a reasonable person of ordinary temperament to lose self-control. Infidelity confessions or direct ocular discovery of adultery legally satisfy the gravity threshold.
- Prong 2 (Suddenness & Cooling-off Interval): The fatal act must be committed immediately upon the provocation before passion cools and self-possession returns. Any intermediate, calculated steps (traveling to a remote depot, procuring a firearm under false pretexts, deliberate transport between locations) legally demonstrate reflection and extinguish the suddenness element.
(3) First Proviso: The provocation must not be sought or voluntarily provoked by the offender as an excuse for killing or doing harm.

### BNS Section 303 — Theft
(1) Whoever, intending to take dishonestly any movable property out of the possession of any person without that person's consent, moves that property in order to such taking, is said to commit theft.
(2) Statutory Elements: (a) Dishonest intention to permanently deprive (animus furandi); (b) Movable property; (c) Taking out of possession of another; (d) Absence of consent; (e) Physical moving of the property.
(3) Punishment: Imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years, or with fine, or with both.

### BNS Section 305 — Theft in Dwelling House or Custody Premises
Whoever commits theft in any building, tent, vessel, office, or depository used as a human dwelling or for the custody of property, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven years, and shall also be liable to fine.

### Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA) Section 104 — Burden of Proof
(1) Whoever desires any Court to give judgment as to any legal right or liability dependent on the existence of facts which he asserts, must prove that those facts exist.
(2) In criminal prosecutions, the State carries the unshifting burden of establishing every statutory ingredient of the offence beyond reasonable doubt.

### Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA) Section 108 — Burden of Proving Exceptions
When a person is accused of any offence, the burden of proving the existence of circumstances bringing the case within any of the General Exceptions in the BNS, or within any special exception or proviso, is upon him, and the Court shall presume the absence of such circumstances until established by the accused on a preponderance of probabilities.

### Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA) Section 61 — Electronic Records
Electronic records are admissible as substantive evidence subject to certification verifying the device integrity, hash authenticity, and continuous chain of custody.
