# Landmark Precedents in Constitutional Law

### Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, (1973) 4 SCC 225
**Bench & Court:** 13-Judge Constitution Bench
**Core Holding:** Propounded the Basic Structure Doctrine: Parliament's amending power under Article 368 is not unlimited and cannot alter the foundational features of the Constitution, such as judicial review, rule of law, and democracy.

### Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India, AIR 1978 SC 597
**Bench & Court:** Supreme Court of India (Bhagwati, J.)
**Core Holding:** Interpreted "procedure established by law" under Article 21 to mean procedure that is just, fair, and reasonable, establishing substantive due process in Indian jurisprudence and interlinking Articles 14, 19, and 21.

### K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India, (2017) 10 SCC 1
**Bench & Court:** 9-Judge Constitution Bench (Chandrachud, J.)
**Core Holding:** Unanimously affirmed that the Right to Privacy is a fundamental right emanating from Article 21 and the freedoms guaranteed under Part III, subject only to proportionality and legitimate state aims.
